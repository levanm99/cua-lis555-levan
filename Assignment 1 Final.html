World Digital Library

System Introduction

Context: The World Digital Library (WDL) is an online catalog that was created in 2009 through a U.S. Library of Congress (LOC) project that aimed to make a wide array of cultural materials accessible to the public online in order to promote cross-cultural curiosity and learning. The LOC; United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO); and international organizations, libraries, museums, and archives all contributed materials to the project from its inception in 2009 until the end of the WDL Charter in 2020. Its goal is to spread intercultural knowledge among educators, scholars, and general users; make more cultural content from diverse backgrounds accessible to users around the globe; and to generally expand content in languages other than English on the Internet. The Library of Congress now runs the World Digital Library as a part of their general website.

Users: The WDL was made for scholars, educators, and curious people around the globe.  The hope was that the multilingual interface would allow people from all different backgrounds to explore the rich cultural materials.

Content: The WDL began with 1,236 items. As of 2015, it had amassed more than 12,000 items from about 200 different countries. As of 2026, it contains a total of 20,940 items. Content is selected based on its cultural and/or historical importance and is meant to represent a wide-range of countries and cultures. Content includes, among other significant cultural materials:

  • Architectural Drawings
  • Films
  • Journals
  • Manuscripts
  • Maps
  • Musical Scores
  • Newspapers
  • Photographs
  • Prints
  • Rare Books
  • Recordings

Each item listed in the system includes the title of the work, the date it was created/published, a list of the material’s contents, detailed notes about the physical make-up of the work, the physical location and call number for the item, headings and genres under which the item can be categorized, and a summary of the work that includes important historical context about the creator and the work itself.

Unique Features

  • Users can download the metadata in each of the seven languages into which they translate the website. This is especially important because the links to the translated pages are now all broken.
  • Users can supposedly see what the old website used to look like by visiting the web archive. However, most links lead to a page that says that the content is not available. Other links lead to web pages entirely written in Arabic.
  • Users can click the "share" button for any of the videos in the collection and get the "Embed Link" which includes the html for the material. This is especially helpful for someone looking to use these materials as part of their webpage design, and people likely do since they are all open access materials.

Pros and Cons of the System

Pros Cons
  • Once the user has made it to the online collection, it is easy to use the filters on the left-hand side to filter results based on categories such as: format, date, subject, language, or library/institute/organization contributor.
  • It is easy to navigate to other works by the same creator or other works from the same time period once a user in on a specific item’s page using the links on the right-hand side.
  • The collection spans a large part of history with items dating back to 1000.
  • The collection also includes a diverse array of primary sources in nearly 65 different languages, some of which are indigenous languages that tend to be underrepresented in other collections.
  • The multilingual aspect is missing–the links to the website translated into Spanish, Portuguese, French, Arabic, Russian, and Chinese takes the user to a page that says “Resource Not Available.” This is a major con, as multilingualism is one of the pillars the WDL was built upon.
  • The layout of the “About this Collection” web page makes it look like the links on the left-hand side are related to the WDL; however, they are simply links that take the user to other parts of the general LOC website.
  • Lesser-known cultures are harder to represent on the online platform because they are often from countries where digitization is less common. These cultures are some of the key cultures that the collection wants to represent, but it can be difficult to locate and digitize the materials.

Suggestions for Improvement

Many of the links on this website are no longer working. For example, the links to the translated webpages in Spanish, Portuguese, French, Arabic, Russian, and Chinese are all broken. A major benefit of a world library is that it has materials from all over the world and can be accessed from all over the world by people of many backgrounds. The fact that the translated websites are no longer available makes the site much less accessible to the users the system originally hoped to serve. I would suggest that the LOC make a concerted effort to go into the WDL and clean up broken links so that it can serve all the populations it served previously.

The page itself is also slightly confusing since it is a part of the overall LOC website. Links that pop up on the left-hand site of the “About this Collection” WDL webpage at first glance seem to be links to redirect the user to other parts of the WDL, but after clicking on them it becomes clear that they redirect the user to the LOC’s main website. I would re-design the page in order to make the search box for the WDL more clear and the links to other LOC pages less prominent.
